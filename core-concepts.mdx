Consensus is an AI search engine for research. A few core ideas explain how it works, and they apply wherever you search: the [web app](/web/overview), the [REST API](/api-reference/overview), or the [MCP server](/mcp/overview).

## The index

Every search runs over a corpus of **more than 200 million peer-reviewed papers**. Consensus aggregates and normalizes metadata from major scholarly sources, including Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, and PubMed, into a single searchable index.

<Info>
  New papers can take time to appear as sources publish and the index refreshes. For time-sensitive topics, treat very recent work as potentially incomplete.
</Info>

## Ranking

Consensus ranks results with **hybrid retrieval**: it combines vector (semantic) search, which matches on meaning, with lexical search, which matches on exact terms. Rather than ranking on text relevance alone, Consensus weights results by:

- **Relevance**: how well the paper answers your query.
- **Study quality**: signals such as study design, journal, and citations.
- **Recency**: how current the work is.

The result is a ranked page where the most relevant, higher-quality, and more current papers surface first.

## Grounded citations

Consensus does not invent citations. Every result maps to a real, indexed paper: citations are retrieved from the corpus, not generated by a language model, and so they are not hallucinated. Each paper links back to the source (by DOI where available), so the evidence behind an answer is verifiable. You can open each paper and read it.

<Note>
  AI summaries are written on top of these real papers, but the papers themselves are retrieved, not fabricated. That is the difference between grounded search and an ungrounded chatbot.
</Note>

## Study types

Consensus classifies papers by study design, so you can target the kind of evidence you need (a randomized trial, a meta-analysis, a case report) and filter to it. Use the `study_types` filter on the [API](/api-reference/filters), or the study type filter in the [web app](/web/searching).

## Filters

Filters narrow a search to the evidence that matters: study type, publication year, journal, open access, sample size, SJR journal quartile, field of study, country, and more. The same filter concepts apply across the web app and the API, so a query you refine in the product translates directly into an API call.

## The Consensus Meter

The [Consensus Meter](/web/understanding-results) aggregates the yes / no / possibly stance of papers on a specific yes-or-no research question, giving you a fast snapshot of where the literature leans.

<Warning>
  The Consensus Meter is a heuristic summary, not a formal meta-analysis. It does not weight studies by methodological rigor the way a systematic review would. Use it to orient yourself, then read the underlying papers before drawing conclusions.
</Warning>

## AI summaries and takeaways

Consensus uses language models to summarize findings: a one-sentence `takeaway` per paper, structured extraction of methods, sample, findings, and limitations, and more. These summaries make large result sets scannable.

<Warning>
  AI summaries are model outputs. They can miss nuance or context, so verify them against the source paper for any high-stakes use. Never rely on them alone for medical, legal, or financial decisions.
</Warning>

## Good to know

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Peer-reviewed by default" icon="shield-check">
    Consensus focuses on peer-reviewed research by default: preprints are excluded unless you opt in. On the API, the `exclude_preprints` filter keeps results peer-reviewed only.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="English first" icon="language">
    The index and ranking are optimized for English-language queries and papers.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Not professional advice" icon="scale-balanced">
    Consensus surfaces research; it does not provide medical, legal, or financial advice. Consult a qualified professional for decisions in those areas.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
