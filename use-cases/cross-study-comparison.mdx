title: "Cross-study comparison"
description: "Query a topic, pull outcome metadata from each study, group by design and population, and highlight where the evidence agrees or diverges."
---

Studies on the same question rarely agree cleanly. You want to see the disagreement structured by who was studied, how, and where.

## How Consensus helps

Each result carries structured metadata you can compare across papers: `study_type`, `sample_size`, `population_type`, and `countries_of_study`. Group on those fields to turn a flat result list into a comparison, then read each `takeaway` to see which direction a group of studies points.

## Recipe

<Steps>
  <Step title="Query the topic">
    Search the question and restrict to comparable designs with `study_types`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fetch results with metadata">
    Pull `study_type`, `sample_size`, `population_type`, and `countries_of_study` from every result.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Group">
    Bucket the papers by design (or by population) so like is compared with like.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Highlight agreement and divergence">
    Within each group, read the takeaways together. Consistent takeaways signal agreement; conflicting ones flag where to dig deeper.
  </Step>
</Steps>

```python
import os, requests
from collections import defaultdict

resp = requests.get(
    "https://api.consensus.app/v1/search",
    headers={"x-api-key": os.environ["CONSENSUS_API_KEY"]},
    params={
        "query": "does cognitive behavioral therapy reduce insomnia severity",
        "study_types": "rct,meta-analysis",
        "human": True,
        "page_size": 15,
    },
)
resp.raise_for_status()
papers = resp.json()["results"]

# Group by study design. Swap the key for population_type to compare cohorts instead.
groups = defaultdict(list)
for paper in papers:
    groups[paper.get("study_type") or "unclassified"].append(paper)

for design, members in groups.items():
    print(f"\n{design}  ({len(members)} papers)")
    for paper in members:
        n = paper.get("sample_size") or "n/a"
        population = paper.get("population_type") or "n/a"
        countries = ", ".join(paper.get("countries_of_study") or []) or "n/a"
        print(f"  - {paper['title'][:70]}")
        print(f"    n={n}, population={population}, countries={countries}")
        print(f"    finding: {paper.get('takeaway') or 'n/a'}")
```

Reading the groups side by side shows the shape of the evidence: whether large trials agree with the meta-analyses, whether findings hold across populations, and whether the studies concentrate in one part of the world.

## Filters that help

- `study_types`: hold design constant so groups are meaningful.
- `sample_size_min`: drop small studies before grouping to reduce noise.
- `human`: restrict to human studies when animal work would muddy the comparison.
- `country`: scope to specific regions with comma-separated ISO codes when geography is the variable of interest.

See the full set in [Filters](/api-reference/filters).

## Tips

- `sample_size` and `study_type` are your two fastest quality signals. A large `rct` and a small case series deserve different weight, even inside the same group.
- `countries_of_study` is a generalizability check. Evidence drawn from a single country may not transfer, and grouping on it makes that visible.
- When groups disagree, the divergence is the finding. Surface it rather than averaging it away, and cite both sides.
