Each result is a real, peer-reviewed paper with a layer of AI-extracted analysis on top. This page explains what that layer contains and how far to trust it.

## Anatomy of a result

### The AI takeaway

Every result leads with a one-sentence takeaway: a plain-language summary of what that specific paper found, grounded in its content. Use it to triage a long results list quickly. In the API this is the `takeaway` field.

### Structured study details

Open a result to see its details extracted into consistent sections:

- **Methods.** How the study was run.
- **Sample.** Who or what was studied, and how many.
- **Findings.** The reported results.
- **Limitations.** Caveats the authors note or the design implies.

Where the question fits a clinical framing, Consensus also extracts **PICO** (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome) and a **study-design confidence** signal that reflects how certain the design classification is.

### Study analysis

A deeper analysis characterizes the study itself, so you can judge its weight at a glance:

- **Design classification:** whether the paper is an RCT, cohort study, review, and so on.
- **Sample size:** the number of participants, or of studies for a review or meta-analysis.
- **Funding and conflict disclosure:** declared funding sources and competing interests, where available.
- **Citation metrics:** how often the paper is cited, and how often by influential work.
- **Open-access links:** a path to a free full-text version when one exists.

Several of these map to fields you can also read from the [API](/api-reference/overview):

| In the result | What it tells you | API field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design classification | The classified study design | `study_type` |
| Sample size | Participants, or studies for a review | `sample_size`, `study_count` |
| Citation metrics | Total citations and influential citations | `citation_count`, `influential_citation_count` |
| Journal quartile | The journal's SJR quartile (Q1 is highest) | `sjr_best_quartile` |
| Preprint status | Whether the paper is a preprint | `is_preprint` |

<Note>
  Some analysis in the web app, including PICO extraction, design confidence, and funding and conflict disclosure, goes beyond the fields the API returns. Check the [API overview](/api-reference/overview) for the exact response schema.
</Note>

## The Consensus Meter

For a yes/no research question, the Consensus Meter aggregates the evidence across the returned papers into a single snapshot: how many support **yes**, how many say **no**, and how many are **possibly** or mixed. It is a fast way to see where the literature leans before you read individual papers.

<Warning>
  The Consensus Meter is a heuristic snapshot, not a formal meta-analysis or systematic review. It summarizes how the returned papers lean; it does not pool effect sizes or formally weight studies by quality. Treat it as orientation, then read the papers before drawing a conclusion.
</Warning>

## Apply your own judgment

Takeaways and summaries are generated by large language models from the source papers. They are grounded in real, cited research, but they are still model outputs.

<Note>
  For high-stakes decisions (clinical, legal, or financial), verify every claim against the original paper. Consensus is a research tool, not medical, legal, or financial advice.
</Note>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why a paper might not appear">
    Consensus is peer-reviewed by default and excludes preprints unless you opt in, so recent or not-yet-reviewed work may be missing. Newly published papers can also take time to be indexed.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Language coverage">
    The index and AI summaries work best with English-language queries and papers, so results skew toward English-language research.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
