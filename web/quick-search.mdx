Quick Search is the default mode and the fastest way to see what the literature says. You ask a research question and get a ranked list of the most relevant papers, each with a one-sentence AI takeaway, so you can scan the evidence without opening every paper.

Results are ranked by a hybrid of relevance, study quality, and recency, drawing on a corpus of over 200 million peer-reviewed papers. For yes/no questions, the [Consensus Meter](/web/consensus-meter) summarizes how many of the top papers say yes, no, or possibly.

## When to use it

Reach for Quick Search when you want speed and breadth over synthesis:

- You are getting oriented on a topic and want to see the key papers fast.
- You have a specific question and just need the most relevant evidence.
- You want to skim takeaways before deciding what to read in full.
- You plan to [filter](/web/filters) the results down by study type, year, or journal quality.

## How it differs from Pro and Deep

Quick Search hands you the papers and their takeaways. It does not write a synthesis across them. When you need the findings connected or reviewed, step up to a deeper mode:

- **[Pro Search](/web/pro-search)** analyzes up to 20 papers and writes a summary that connects findings across them.
- **[Deep review](/web/deep-review)** breaks a complex question into sub-questions and produces a structured literature review across up to 50 papers.

<Tip>
  Start in Quick Search to map the evidence, then rerun the same question in Pro or Deep when you want the papers synthesized rather than just listed.
</Tip>

## Narrow your results

Quick Search pairs well with [filters](/web/filters). Constrain by study type, publication year, journal, journal quality, sample size, field of study, country, or open access to focus the ranked list on the evidence you trust.
