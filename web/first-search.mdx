---
title: "Your first search"
description: "Run your first research question on Consensus and read the results in a couple of minutes."
---

You can run your first search in about a minute. You do not need an account to try it, though signing in gives you more results per search and saves your [search history](/web/search-history).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Consensus" icon="globe">
    Go to [consensus.app](https://consensus.app) and you can start searching right away. To get more results per search, save your history, and unlock deeper modes, [create a free account](https://consensus.app/sign-up/).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask a research question" icon="magnifying-glass">
    Type your question the way you would ask a colleague. Consensus understands natural language, so you do not need keywords or Boolean operators, though those work too.

    A few tips for a strong first query:

    - Ask one specific question rather than typing a bare topic.
    - Name the key elements: the population, the intervention or exposure, and the outcome you care about.
    - Keep it to a single question. You can dig into follow-ups afterward.

    <Tip>
      Specific beats broad. Instead of `intermittent fasting`, ask `Does intermittent fasting improve insulin sensitivity in adults with type 2 diabetes?` The more precise your question, the more precisely Consensus can rank the evidence.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the ranked results" icon="list-check">
    Consensus returns a ranked list of the most relevant papers, each with a one-sentence AI takeaway summarizing what that paper found. Scan the takeaways to get the shape of the evidence fast.

    For yes/no questions, the [Consensus Meter](/web/consensus-meter) shows how many of the top papers say yes, no, or possibly, so you can gauge the balance of evidence at a glance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open a paper" icon="book-open">
    Click any result to open its [Study Snapshot](/web/study-snapshot): the study design, sample size, population, and key findings, alongside the abstract. From there you can follow the citation to the source paper by its DOI, or save the paper to your [library](/web/library).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Too many results, or the wrong kind? Narrow them with [filters](/web/filters) such as study type, publication year, and journal quality.
</Tip>
