The Citation Graph turns a paper's citation network into an interactive map. Instead of reading a flat reference list, you can see how a paper connects to the wider literature and follow those links to discover foundational and related work.

## What the graph shows

A citation network links papers through the work they cite and the work that cites them:

- **Foundational work**: the earlier papers a study builds on, which point you to the origins of an idea.
- **Connected work**: other papers linked through the citation network, which help you branch out to related research.

Following these links is often the fastest way to move from one relevant paper to the cluster of work around it.

## Open the Citation Graph

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a starting paper">
    Choose a paper from your search results to anchor the graph.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open its Citation Graph">
    Open the Citation Graph for that paper to see its network laid out visually, with the paper you chose at the center.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Find your way around

From the anchor paper at the center, you can:

- **Follow a connection**: select a linked paper to see how it relates, then keep following links to trace an idea across the literature.
- **Open a paper**: when one looks relevant, open it to read its [Study Snapshot](/web/study-snapshot) and takeaway.
- **Branch out**: use any paper you land on as a new starting point to widen your search.

<Tip>
  Use the Citation Graph when you have one strong paper and want more like it. It surfaces the foundational and connected work that a keyword search might miss.
</Tip>
