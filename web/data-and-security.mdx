Consensus indexes peer-reviewed literature and uses AI to summarize and analyze it. This page explains what that means for how you read the results, and where to go for detail on the dataset, security, and AI usage.

## What Consensus indexes

Consensus searches a corpus of over 200 million peer-reviewed papers, sourced from Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, and PubMed. Results are peer-reviewed by default. For more on the corpus and how results are ranked, see [The corpus](/web/corpus).

## AI summaries are a starting point

The one-sentence takeaways, Pro summaries, and Deep review write-ups are generated by large language models reading the underlying papers. They are a fast way to grasp the evidence, but they are AI outputs.

<Warning>
  For any high-stakes use, verify AI summaries against the cited papers before you rely on them. Follow the citation to the source and confirm the claim in the paper itself.
</Warning>

## The Consensus Meter is a heuristic

For yes/no questions, the [Consensus Meter](/web/consensus-meter) aggregates how many of the top papers say yes, no, or possibly. It is a heuristic snapshot to gauge the state of the evidence, not a formal meta-analysis. Read it as a quick orientation, then look at the papers themselves.

## Not professional advice

<Warning>
  Consensus is a research tool, not a substitute for a professional. It is not medical, legal, or financial advice. Consult a qualified professional for decisions in those areas.
</Warning>
