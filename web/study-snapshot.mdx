The Study Snapshot pulls the details that matter from a paper into a single per-paper view, so you can judge how much weight to give it without opening the full text.

## What a snapshot shows

For a single paper, a snapshot brings together:

- **Study design and type**: the kind of study, for example a randomized controlled trial, cohort study, case report, or systematic review. Design tells you how much causal weight the findings can carry.
- **Sample size**: how many participants or observations the study is based on. Larger, well-powered samples generally support more reliable conclusions.
- **Population**: who or what was studied, for example humans or animals, and the characteristics of the participants. This tells you how well the findings transfer to the group you care about.
- **Findings**: the paper's key result, summarized so you can see its conclusion at a glance.
- **Study-quality signals**: additional signals that speak to the strength of the evidence, so you can weigh a paper in context rather than by its title alone.

## Judge a paper quickly

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start with the design">
    A finding from a randomized controlled trial or systematic review usually carries more weight than one from a small observational study or a single case report.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the sample and population">
    Confirm the study was large enough to be meaningful and was run in a population that matches your question.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the finding in context">
    Match the paper's finding against the rest of your results. The [Consensus Meter](/web/consensus-meter) shows how it fits the broader split of yes, no, and possibly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Use [filters](/web/filters) to hold your whole results list to the same bar, for example only randomized controlled trials or only studies above a minimum sample size.
</Tip>
