Deep review, also called Deep Search, is the most thorough mode. It produces a comprehensive literature review across up to 50 papers. Instead of answering in one pass, it breaks your question into sub-questions, runs up to roughly 20 targeted searches, and synthesizes a structured review that surfaces major findings and counterpoints. Deep review is powered by the [Research Agent](/web/research-agent) and supports [Threads](/web/threads) for follow-ups.

## When to use it

Deep review is built for complex topics that a single search cannot cover:

- Your question has several parts or spans more than one subfield.
- You want a structured overview of what the literature says, including where it disagrees.
- You are scoping a literature review, a grant, or a manuscript and need breadth with citations.
- You want major findings and counterpoints surfaced, not just the top-ranked papers.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Breaks your question into sub-questions">
    Deep review decomposes a complex prompt into the distinct questions it needs to answer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Runs targeted searches">
    It runs up to roughly 20 targeted searches, each aimed at a different part of your question, across the corpus.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reads and compares papers">
    The [Research Agent](/web/research-agent) reads and compares across up to 50 papers, using tools and academic filters to find the strongest evidence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Synthesizes a structured review">
    It writes a structured review that surfaces major findings and counterpoints, with grounded citations throughout.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The analysis can also render diagrams, including [Mermaid diagrams](/web/diagrams-in-analysis), to map relationships or processes in the evidence.

## Keep going with Threads

A Deep review is a starting point for a conversation, not a dead end. [Threads](/web/threads) let you ask follow-up questions within the same review while keeping its context, so you can drill into a sub-question or a specific paper without rerunning everything.

## How it differs from Quick and Pro

- **[Quick Search](/web/quick-search)** returns ranked papers with one-sentence takeaways for a fast scan.
- **[Pro Search](/web/pro-search)** connects findings across up to 20 papers in a single synthesis, without breaking the question into sub-questions.
