Consensus searches a corpus of **over 200 million peer-reviewed papers**. Every search runs against this index, so its coverage and quality shape what you find.

## Where the papers come from

Consensus draws its papers from three established scholarly sources:

- [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org)
- [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org)
- [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)

Together they span a wide range of research fields.

## Peer-reviewed by default

The corpus is peer-reviewed by default. Preprints are excluded unless you choose to include them, so your results emphasize work that has been through peer review.

Consensus is also optimized for English-language search, so English queries and papers return the best results.

<Note>
  New papers can take time to appear in the index. If a very recent publication is missing, indexing lag is the usual reason.
</Note>

## How results are ranked

Consensus ranks results by weighing three signals together:

- **Relevance** to your question
- **Study quality**
- **Recency**

For how ranking and grounded citations fit together, see [How Consensus works](/web/how-it-works).

## Search a slice of the corpus

To focus on part of the corpus, such as a study type, a date range, or higher-ranked journals, apply [Advanced filters](/web/filters).
